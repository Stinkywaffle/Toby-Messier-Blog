Life as an Amazon Driver

What it's like to deliver hundreds of packages a day as an Amazon Driver.

By Toby Messier

What Is This Job?

Amazon doesn't deliver packages itself — they contract it out to smaller companies called Delivery Service Partners (DSPs). I work for ASLAR Logistics, a DSP based out of Sumner, WA. We take vans full of Amazon packages and spend the day delivering them across assigned routes.

Every day is a bit different, but loadout is the same. You show up to the Amazon station, get handed a route with anywhere from 100 to 250 stops, load up your van and leave. The app tells you where to go and what packages to deliver. The job is to execute it as efficiently as possible.

A Typical Day

No two days are exactly the same, but the structure is consistent. Here's how a shift plays out:

  • 9:45 AM Clock in and get van/route assignment.
  • 9:55 AM Safety check-in.
  • 10:00 AM Head to loading pads.
  • 10:15 AM Load van.
  • 10:30 AM Head to the route.
  • 11:00 AM – 7:30 PM Deliver packages. (Lately, I've been banished to apartments in Des Moines.)
  • 7:30 PM Return to station.
  • 8:00 PM Final van inspection / return undelivered packages.
  • 8:15 PM Clock out.

How Each Stop Works

Most people picture delivering a package as just dropping a box on a doorstep — and sometimes it's exactly that. But there's a specific process for every stop:

  1. Navigate to the address
    • The Rabbit app routes you automatically — no manual input needed
    • You can override the order if it makes more geographic sense
    • Some addresses have special access codes or gate instructions in the notes
  2. Find the package
    • Packages have a three digit number that drivers use to know which package goes to which house. Sometimes you have a multi-stop, which is multiple houses or apartments classified as one stop.
  3. Scan packages
  4. Deliver the package to the corresponding house and take a picture for proof of delivery

Stories from the Road

After hundreds of hours on the road, you collect stories. Here are a few of my favorites:

Story 01

Dog Encounters

When you arrive to a location, sometimes Amazon will let you know that there are dogs reported at the location, so it's always important to get out and check before making the delivery. One time, I arrived at a house in the middle of nowhere and got that notification. So I looked around for a dog and didn't notice anything. I got out of the van and got to the door, and there was a massive pig blocking my way. The pig didn't do anything or get mad at me, but it definitely scared me.

Story 02

25 Stops, One Building

One day, I got my route in the morning, and it showed I only had 25 stops, which I thought was crazy. I load up and drive to my first location, only to find out that all 25 of my stops that day would be in the same apartment complex. They had a massive locker room, and every single package in my van would go there today. I was there for about 5 hours, just chugging along and listening to my music. I was enjoying myself until my boss called near the end and told me that when I finished the apartment, I was to start driving to other drivers and taking packages from them to help them finish their routes.

Story 03

Running on Empty

One day, I was driving a rented electric vehicle that had a range of 100 miles. My location was about 20 miles from the warehouse. I was going about my day, and near 5:00, I realized I only had a range of 32 miles left. I still had 50-60 stops left and definitely wouldn't make it. So I called our dispatch team, and, come to find out, I was supposed to advise them when I got to 50%. So I then sat in my van for an hour while dispatch figured out if I should drive back or not. That day, I ended up returning about a third of my packages.
